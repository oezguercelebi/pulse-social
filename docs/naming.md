# Naming

> **Decision: the product is named Kin.** This document records the rationale and the runner-up candidates considered.

Working name **Pulse** doesn't fit. "Pulse" is a metric — heart rate, engagement, validation signal. The product is the opposite: a network that measures connection, not pulse. We chose a name that says *your people, intentional warmth, human-scale*.

## What the name should do

- Suggest **smallness, intimacy, human-scale** — not a platform, not a network in the cold sense.
- Feel **timeless**, not techy. We're not "AI Social v2"; we're a place.
- Be **short** (1–2 syllables) and **easy to type and say**.
- Feel **warm** without being twee.
- Survive being said next to "LinkedIn," "Twitter," "Facebook" without sounding smaller-than. (e.g. "I posted on ___ this morning.")

## What the name should *not* do

- Reference pulse, beat, signal, feed, stream, flow, ping, buzz — any *engagement metric* word. That's exactly what we're against.
- Sound like a 2014 startup ("ly" suffix, vowel-dropped, etc.).
- Sound like a dating app or a meditation app.
- Be ungoogleable or hard to spell.

## Candidates

### Top picks

**1. Hearth**
A gathering place. Warmth. Where you have your people over. Anti-cold-feed by definition. The metaphor is doing real work — a hearth is *intentionally small*, *durable*, and *for people you've chosen to invite*.
- Strengths: distinctive, warm, reads well as a verb-adjacent noun ("on Hearth"), strong domain story (`hearth.app`, `hearth.so`, etc.).
- Weakness: slightly precious; needs confident design to not feel twee.

**2. Kin**
Three letters. Means *your people*, by blood or by choice. Says intimacy without saying anything else. "I saw it on Kin." "I'm on Kin." Reads clean as a verb-adjacent identity.
- Strengths: shortest possible name, instantly understood, no platform-baggage.
- Weakness: very common word — SEO and trademark are work; `kin.com` is taken (acquired by Microsoft for Kin phone, decade-defunct, but the trademark is sticky); plenty of `kin`-prefixed startups exist.

**3. Atrium**
A gathering space, open to the sky, in the middle of a building. Architectural, communal, light. Suggests an open commons (good for the open-source story) without being pretentious.
- Strengths: distinctive, evocative, connotes *openness with intention*. Good visual identity potential.
- Weakness: 3 syllables (longer than Hearth/Kin); "atrium" is also a heart chamber, slight overlap with the *Pulse* mistake; "Atrium" the legal-services startup exists but is dormant.

### Strong alternates

**4. Folk**
Your folks, your folk. Warm, plain, slightly Americana. Plural-collective baked into the meaning.
- Strengths: short, friendly, hard to mistake for a tech-bro name.
- Weakness: generic word, weak for trademark; "Folk" calendar app already exists.

**5. Ember**
Small intimate fire. Lasting warmth. Companion to Hearth thematically.
- Strengths: distinctive, evocative, one cleanly visual brand asset.
- Weakness: Ember.js the framework still has SEO weight; "Ember" used by several other apps.

**6. Coterie**
A small intimate group of friends with a shared interest. Literally the dictionary definition of what we're building.
- Strengths: most semantically precise candidate. Unusual = memorable.
- Weakness: 3 syllables, slightly precious, feels French in an exclusive way (which could be on-brand or off-brand depending on framing).

**7. Common** / **Commons**
The shared space. Strong open-source signal. Anti-private-platform.
- Strengths: principled, on-thesis (commons = collectively owned).
- Weakness: very generic, hard to own; "Commons" already used by several apps.

### Wildcards

**8. Verge**
Frontier, edge, threshold. We're at the edge of a new social paradigm.
- Weakness: "The Verge" media brand owns this word.

**9. Bench**
The folks who sit together on the bench — your team, your starters, your ride-or-dies. Sports/working metaphor.
- Strengths: warm, plain, distinctive in the social-app space.
- Weakness: "Bench" accounting / "BenchSci" already exist; mixed connotation.

**10. Folio**
A collection. A portfolio. Intentional curation.
- Weakness: feels more "creator portfolio" than "social network."

**11. Glade**
A clearing in the forest where people gather. Quiet, intentional, natural.
- Strengths: distinctive, calm-feeling, on-thesis.
- Weakness: 1 syllable but unusual word; might read fantasy/twee.

**12. Pier**
A dock. Public structure, where people gather to meet others coming and going. Open architecture.
- Weakness: maritime overload; "Pier" the password manager exists.

## Decision: Kin (network) + Ari (default agent name)

The product is **Kin**. Every user's agent is named **Ari** by default, customizable. Two names, two roles:

- **Kin** describes the *place*. "I'm on Kin." "Find me on Kin." Network-shaped grammar.
- **Ari** describes the *agent*. "Ari drafted this for me." "My Ari runs the digest." Person-shaped grammar.

This avoids the trap of using one name to do both jobs. Assistant names (Siri, Alexa, Aria, Cortana) read as people; network names (Twitter, Discord, LinkedIn) read as places. Conflating them — calling the whole product Ari — would imply Kin is *an agent*, when the thesis is that Kin is *a network where every member has one*.

Apple/Siri is the model: brand is the company-place, assistant has its own name and personality.

## Why Kin over the alternates

Three letters. Means *your people*, by blood or by choice. Says intimacy without saying anything else. Reads clean: "I'm on Kin." "Find me on Kin." "We met on Kin."

Why Kin over Hearth and Atrium:
- **Shortest possible name.** Three letters is a permanent advantage in URL bars, app icons, conversation, and headlines.
- **Instant comprehension.** No one needs to be taught what "kin" means. Hearth has a slight twee risk; Atrium leans literary. Kin is plain in the right way.
- **Plain enough to live next to "Twitter," "LinkedIn," "Facebook"** without sounding smaller-than.
- **Says the thesis directly.** The product is about people who actually care — not followers, not connections, not contacts. Kin.

### Open work the name implies

- **Trademark.** "Kin" is a common word; there are non-conflicting prior uses (the long-defunct Microsoft Kin phone among them) and several `kin`-prefixed companies in adjacent spaces. A USPTO TESS search in IC 042 (software) and IC 045 (social) should be done before committing to logo and marks.
- **Domain.** `kin.com` is taken. `kin.app`, `kin.so`, `kin.social`, `kin.network`, `getkin.com`, `kin.is` should be checked. We don't need `kin.com` to launch.
- **Verb form.** "On Kin" works as the dominant grammar. We don't force a verb ("kin me" is awkward). "Find me on Kin" is plenty.
- **Visual identity.** Three letters give us strong typographic options — the wordmark *is* the logo. Lean into that.
