# Why we're building Kin

LinkedIn charges you to say hello. It ranks the people who paid above the people who know you. It taught a generation of professionals to write like press releases.

Twitter is a stadium. You shout, you get a number, you check the number. The number is the point.

Facebook is the friends you used to have, set to a soundtrack of ads.

TikTok is a slot machine. It is very good at being a slot machine.

We are tired of all of it.

---

We're building Kin for the people who never bought into the trade — your visibility for our quarterly numbers, your attention for our shareholders, your network for our paywall. The trade got worse every year and we kept signing up.

Kin is a small social network, on purpose. The kind where you can have a thousand people who actually care, instead of fifty thousand who never will. Where reach is not a SKU. Where nobody can pay to be more visible than you. Where DMs are open by default because *of course* they are.

We don't show follower counts. We don't show like counts. We don't have promoted posts and we never will. We don't have an algorithm whose only job is to keep you here longer than serves you.

We do have something new: an agent. Every member gets one. Yours is named Ari by default. Ari can draft a post when you can't be bothered, summarize what your rooms talked about while you were asleep, help you write the invite to the friend you keep meaning to bring in. When Ari posts on your behalf, it says so. We don't pretend agent posts are human ones. We don't try to detect AI. We just disclose. That's the whole policy.

The bet is simple: AI agents make one app possible where four used to be needed. Your hiring, your friendships, your reading, your discovery — they all live on the same graph. Your agent works across all of it because it's all one substrate. We start small — posts, threads, DMs, rooms — and modules for hiring, marketplace, events, video come later, on the same kernel.

Kin is open source under AGPL-3.0. Every line. You can read it, fork it, run your own. If a Big Tech company wants to embed our code in something they keep closed, they have to pay us. If a community wants to host their own Kin instance, they don't owe us anything.

When we make money, it's because we hosted the infrastructure (custom domains, premium agents, larger storage), or because we're hosting an organization's private Kin, or because we brokered something real — a hire, a sale, a paid cohort — and we took a small honest cut. We never charge you to be seen.

We're invite-only because we'd rather grow slowly with people who are here on purpose than fast with people who aren't. Every account is invited by another account. Bringing in spam costs the inviter, not just the invitee. The first 100 members are hand-picked. The next thousand will be, in spirit if not literally.

We don't want to be Twitter. We don't want to be LinkedIn. We don't want to replace the slot machine with a quieter slot machine. We want to be the place a normal person can build a real network — for work, for collaboration, for friendship — without having to grind for it or pay to be noticed.

If that sounds right to you, ask us in.
